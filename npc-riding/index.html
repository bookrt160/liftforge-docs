    NPC riding guide¶

    LiftForge can let AI characters use cars on their own: they call the car, wait politely, board, press their floor, ride and get out. There are two ways to use it: add navigation links and use a plain Move To, or call a ride task from a Behaviour Tree, a StateTree or a Blueprint.

    The sample NPC and the NPC demo described in section 10 are part of the LF_Showcase_Warm and LF_Showcase_Noir maps: press Play and watch the first three cars.

    1. What NPC riding does¶

    The NPC code is a separate module, LiftForgeAI, so the core runtime stays free of AI dependencies. The plugin enables the engine plugins StateTree and GameplayStateTree for it.

    A ride goes through five phases:

    1. Approaching. The NPC walks to a waiting spot beside the landing door. Far landings use the AI controller's pathfinding when there is a navigation mesh.
    2. Waiting. The NPC stands at its waiting spot and presses the call button again every 2 seconds while no serving car stands open there. If a car stands open there but still shows the other direction (it has just brought somebody from that side) and has no more stops that way, the NPC presses the button of its own direction once, as a person would, and the car takes that direction. If a crowd pushes it into the light curtain, it boards on its turn or steps straight out.
    3. Boarding. The NPC walks in along the door axis, once the doors have been open for a short moment and nobody is getting out.
    4. Riding. The NPC presses its floor, stands on a free cabin spot clear of the doors and turns to the door it will leave by.
    5. Exiting. The NPC walks out along the door axis and to a spot beside the door.

    A ride runs on the server only. See section 9.

    2. Ways to make an NPC ride¶

    Way Where Result
    Elevator Nav Links (component) Add to a placed car A plain Move To across floors works. Pathfinding plans through the car, the AI rides and walks on.
    Ride Elevator To Floor (Behaviour Tree task) Behaviour Trees Succeeds when the pawn is out of the car on the floor, fails otherwise.
    Ride Elevator To Floor (StateTree task, category LiftForge) StateTrees Running while riding, Succeeded or Failed at the end.
    AI Ride Elevator (latent Blueprint node) Any Blueprint Exec pins Arrived and Failed. Exactly one fires, once.

    All of them drive the same component, Elevator Ride (LFElevatorRideComponent). It is added to the pawn automatically; place it yourself to tune it.

    3. Navigation links (a plain Move To)¶

    1. Select a placed car and Add Component > Elevator Nav Links.
    2. Rebuild navigation (Build > Build Paths) and save the map. Link ids are generated in the editor and saved with the component, so a navigation mesh built in the editor finds its links in a cooked game.
    3. Use a normal Move To to a point on another floor. The path goes through the car: the link pauses the move, runs a ride, lets the pawn out exactly at the link end and resumes the move. A failed ride aborts the move, and the AI's Move To fails so your logic can re-plan.

    Setup notes:

    • Put a NavMeshBoundsVolume around every landing of every floor, plus about 2 m. Agent radius 34 to 42 cm and agent height at least 180 cm are suitable. A static navigation mesh is fine: the links are baked.
    • The component makes one link per pair of floors, in both directions, between landing points LandingDistance (120 cm) out from the landing doors. Keep the landing walkable at least that far.
    • Link cost is the vertical distance plus an entering cost (600, about six metres of walking, for the wait). Short hops prefer stairs where there are any.
    • Keep bExcludeCabinFromNavigation on (default). In game, the cabin parts and landing door leaves then stop affecting navigation, so a runtime-built navigation mesh is not rebuilt while the car moves.
    • After you change floors at runtime, call Refresh Links.
    • Availability. Links of a car that cannot serve (a fault, any mode other than Normal; for a bank, when no member can serve) are skipped by new path queries.
    • Access levels. A link to or from a floor with an access level is used only by an AI whose Elevator Ride component has Default Access Level at least that level (staff, guards). For everyone else pathfinding finds another way, or none. DefaultAccessLevel is 0 by default and is also the level of rides that links start.

    Properties: LinkAreaClass, LandingDistance, CallMode, RideTimeoutSeconds, bExcludeCabinFromNavigation, SupportedAgents. Functions: Get Elevator, Get Link Count, Refresh Links.

    4. Behaviour Tree¶

    Add the task Ride Elevator To Floor to a sequence. It succeeds when the pawn is out of the car on the floor and fails otherwise; aborting the task cancels the ride.

    Option Meaning
    ElevatorKey An object key holding a car or a bank. Empty: the nearest car that serves the floor.
    DestinationFloor or DestinationFloorKey The floor, as a fixed value or a name key.
    AccessLevel The access level used for the ride.
    CallMode See section 7.
    TimeoutSeconds 0 for no limit.

    A minimal tree: Sequence [ Ride Elevator To Floor (destination floor key), Move To (room) ].

    5. StateTree¶

    Add the task Ride Elevator To Floor (category LiftForge) to a state of a StateTree that uses the schema "StateTree AI Component". Inputs: Pawn (binds automatically to the AI schema's actor), an optional ElevatorOrBank, and parameters DestinationFloor, AccessLevel, CallMode, TimeoutSeconds. Outputs: Failure and RejectReason. The task is Running while it rides and finishes with Succeeded or Failed; leaving the state cancels the ride.

    A minimal tree: Idle (Delay) → Ride (task) → Walk (Move To).

    6. Blueprint node¶

    AI Ride Elevator is a latent node. Exec pins: Arrived (Rider, Elevator, Floor Id) and Failed (Rider, Elevator, Failure, Reject Reason). Exactly one fires, once. Cancel ends the ride with Cancelled.

    The failure is a ELFRideFailure: InvalidRequest, CallRejected, ServiceUnavailable, Interrupted, Blocked (could not reach the landing, the car or the exit in time, or could not board within BoardStallSeconds), TimedOut, Cancelled, ElevatorDestroyed. For CallRejected the second output is the ELFCallRejectReason.

    If you want to drive the ride yourself, use the Elevator Ride component: Start Ride (elevator, destination floor, origin floor, access level, call mode, timeout), Cancel Ride, and the events On Ride Arrived, On Ride Failed and On Ride Phase Changed, with status getters (Is Riding, Get Ride Phase, and so on).

    7. Queueing, doors, load and faults¶

    Queueing and doors

    • Waiting spots alternate left and right of the door, and are placed clear of the light curtain.
    • People get out first. A rider waits until the doors have been open for BoardDelaySeconds, the curtain is free and no rider getting off at this floor is still inside. It then boards in the same door opening: it decides whether the car is full only after those riders are out, so a car with a single cabin spot works too. A player, or a pawn without the Elevator Ride component, counts only while it stands in the door curtain, so an NPC can board while such a pawn is still inside.
    • One rider goes through the door at a time, and first come boards first.
    • On the way out, the rider nearest the door goes first and presses Door Open if the doors start closing.
    • A rider that is not yet in the doorway gives up boarding when the doors start to close: an NPC does not wait in the doorway. Riders inside stand clear of the curtain, so the doors close after the normal dwell.

    Load. A rider boards only when the car's load, the riders already walking in and its own mass fit the capacity, and a free cabin spot exists. Otherwise it lets the car go and waits for the next one. A rider counts its LFLoadComponent mass, or the car's Passenger Mass Kg. A 220 × 240 cm cabin takes 4 NPCs of radius 34 cm; extra NPCs take the next car, even when the capacity would allow more.

    Banks and call modes. Set CallMode on the task, the component or the links:

    • Hall Call (default). The NPC presses the landing button for its direction and the destination inside. With a bank, the call goes through the bank and the NPC takes any member car that opens for its direction. With destination dispatch the NPC boards only the car the bank assigned.
    • Destination Dispatch. The NPC asks the bank for its destination and boards only the assigned car. Without a bank, it calls the car with Call To Floor.
    • This Car Only. The call goes to this car even when it belongs to a bank. This also rides a car in Independent mode when an attendant opens it.

    Rejections and faults. These end the ride at once: unknown floor, same floor, invalid direction, access denied, invalid configuration. Any other refusal, or a car that stops serving (fault, out of service, maintenance, fire recall, independent service, resume required), is waited out for ServiceWaitSeconds (60 by default), then the ride fails with Service Unavailable (outside the car) or Interrupted (inside). If the car opens its doors while it is not serving the trip (Out of Service letting passengers out, fire recall), the rider gets out there and the ride ends Interrupted with the reason. In a Maintenance stop the doors stay shut, so a rider inside presses Door Open, gets out, and the ride ends Interrupted.

    Characters and other pawns. Characters ride on the cabin floor as their movement base. The component steers them with movement input. Other pawns (no CharacterMovement) are carried by the component while inside.

    Tuning (defaults, on the Elevator Ride component): AcceptanceRadius 25 cm, WaitDistance 70 cm, WaitSideClearance 35 cm, ExitDistance 170 cm, BoardDelaySeconds 1.2, BoardStallSeconds 45, FaceDoorDegreesPerSecond 270, BoardTimeoutSeconds 8, ApproachTimeoutSeconds 60, ExitTimeoutSeconds 10, ServiceWaitSeconds 60, RetryIntervalSeconds 2, MoveInputScale 1, bUseNavigationToApproach on, DefaultAccessLevel 0.

    8. What you can use without the NPC module¶

    An AI pawn can also use a car with the public API alone: Call To Floor, the latent Move Elevator To Floor node, the door and obstruction events, Get Occupants, the call-rejection reason, and Request Destination on a bank. The NPC module adds the walking, queueing and boarding on top of these. See Blueprint API and Bank and destination dispatch.

    9. Multiplayer¶

    Riding is server only. Start Ride on a client logs a warning and fails with Invalid Request. NPCs call the car on the server's actors directly with their own access level: no interactor component, no network request. Clients see NPCs through normal Character replication and see the cars through the replicated state.

    Check Has Authority before running NPC decision logic. The elevator events now also fire on clients. See the Multiplayer guide. Give NPC characters bStayBasedInAir if they can jump.

    10. The example NPCs in the showcase maps¶

    Each example map has six sample NPCs that walk between the floors and ride the cars on their own. Press Play and watch the first three cars.

    • The NPC. LiftForge Content/Examples/AI/BP_LF_SampleNPC is a Blueprint of LiftForge Sample NPC (ALFSampleNPC, a Character with an Elevator Ride component, UE's default 34 x 88 cm capsule and a simple body mesh). Its controller, LiftForge Sample NPC Controller, waits 2 to 6 seconds, picks a target point on another floor and walks there with a plain Move To. The cars do the rest through their Elevator Nav Links. It does not use a Behaviour Tree or a StateTree, so you can see that no elevator logic is needed in the AI.
    • The cars. The Traction car and the two MRL cars carry the Elevator Nav Links component. The Traction car and the first MRL car use hall calls; the second MRL car uses destination dispatch.
    • The places. Thirteen target points (tag LiftForgeNPCTarget) stand on the landings in front of those cars.
    • Navigation. A navigation bounds volume covers the three lobbies and a RecastNavMesh actor with Runtime Generation = Dynamic builds the mesh when the level starts, so nothing baked can go out of date.
    • Multiplayer. The map has four player starts, so a listen server with several clients works at once (Play > Number of Players 2+, Net Mode Play As Listen Server). The NPCs run on the server only. In the release check (a listen server and two clients, 100 ms round trip, 1 % packet loss, UE 5.6, 5.7 and 5.8), NPCs riding a moving car stayed on its floor on both clients: the largest frame-to-frame change of an NPC's height above the cabin floor was 0.7 cm, and the spread over a ride under 1.6 cm.
    • Known limit. In a test on the Warm map (30 runs of 230 s, UE 5.6), 314 rides of the sample NPCs arrived and 1 failed. No rider got stuck while getting out of a car. The one failure was Blocked: the rider waited in front of an open car and did not board in time. The cause of that case is not known. Your own logic should expect a Blocked result and give the NPC a new goal.

    The sample NPC uses the defaults of the Elevator Ride component and the engine's default Character capsule (34 x 88 cm). It is not shrunk to fit. The showcase lobby walls stand 46 cm in front of the landing doors, with an opening only 30 cm wider than the door. A rider finds its way there without tuning: when the waiting spot (or the way from it to the door) would touch level geometry, the Elevator Ride component moves the spot out in steps of 10 cm, up to 150 cm, until a pawn of its size fits and can walk sideways to the door axis and straight in; it then lines up with the door before it enters, instead of cutting the corner of the wall. Static and dynamic level geometry counts; other pawns and the car itself do not. Wait Distance is the starting point of that search, so you rarely need to change it; raise it yourself if your recess is deeper than 1.5 m or you want the riders to stand further back.

    Character size and door width¶

    A rider fits a car when its capsule is narrower than the door: 2 x radius + 6 cm must not exceed the clear door width (Door Width of the configuration). The release check (LiftForge.Content.StandardCharacterRides) puts a plain Character in front of each of the nine showcase cars and rides it from the first to the second floor:

    Preset Door width Largest radius (rule) 34 x 88 (UE default) 42 x 96
    Traction, MRL 1000, Panoramic, Hydraulic, Platform 90 cm 42 cm rides rides
    MRL 630, Cycle 80 cm 37 cm rides does not fit (84 cm capsule, 80 cm door)
    Bed 2500 130 cm 62 cm rides rides
    Goods 2000 140 cm 67 cm rides rides

    The cabin spots also depend on the radius: a 110 x 140 cm cabin (MRL 630) has room for one rider of radius 34 cm clear of the door light curtain, so a second NPC takes the next car. If your character is wider than the door of a preset, widen Door Width (and the cabin) of your configuration; the NPC module does not squeeze a capsule through a door.

    To build your own: a Character with an AI controller, an Elevator Nav Links component on every car, a navigation bounds volume over the landings, and a Behaviour Tree that does Move To to a point on another floor, or the Ride Elevator To Floor task, or the AI Ride Elevator node.

    11. Troubleshooting¶

    NPCs never use the elevator. Check that the Elevator Nav Links component is on the car, that navigation was rebuilt after you added it, that the link ends are on the navigation mesh (check LandingDistance), and that the car is in Normal mode without a fault.

    The NPC waits for ever. The car may not serve the floor or direction. Check ServiceWaitSeconds, BoardStallSeconds and whether someone stands in the door. With BoardStallSeconds at 0 a rider that cannot board waits for ever.

    The NPC stands next to the door. Something stands on the waiting spots or in front of the door. The ride steers straight in the lobby and does not path-find around people. Keep 2 m beside and 3 m in front of the doors free, and have the sample NPC walk away after it arrives.

    The navigation mesh rebuilds while the car moves. Keep bExcludeCabinFromNavigation on.

    The NPC refuses a floor. It is access denied: raise the NPC's DefaultAccessLevel or the task's access level. The failure reads Call Rejected with the reason Access Denied.

    The NPC does not ride to the right floor. Check the destination floor id and that the car serves it (Get Floors). Unknown floors fail at once with Invalid Request.
