    Using your own character¶

    The demo character and VR pawn are samples. You do not need them. Any pawn can ride a car and press its buttons.

    How riding works¶

    • Characters (CharacterMovement). The cabin has a floor component (Get Cabin Floor returns it). A character standing on it moves with the car through CharacterMovement's normal moving-base handling. You do not add anything to the character for this.
    • Other pawns (a VR pawn, a vehicle, a custom pawn without CharacterMovement). Add a LFRiderComponent. It looks below the owner for a cabin floor (Floor Probe Distance, 120 cm by default) and carries the pawn with it. The pawn needs a collidable root, and cameras and controllers should sit below a tracking-origin component.
    • The cabin floor component is kept when the car rebuilds, so riders stay based on it.
    • The hoistway has invisible blocking walls (Shaft Collision, on by default), open only at the landing entrances. They block pawns and physics bodies, so a character cannot walk into the shaft or fall into the pit.

    Multiplayer settings are in the next section.

    Checklist for a custom character¶

    1. Collision. The generated cabin parts block every channel except Camera, and the shaft walls block the Pawn and PhysicsBody channels. A standard Character capsule (Pawn object type) collides with both. If you changed your character's collision profile, make sure it still blocks WorldStatic and WorldDynamic.
    2. Movement mode. Use the standard Walking movement mode. In single player no special moving-base setup is needed on the character. For multiplayer, apply the settings below.
    3. Pressing buttons. Use the trace helper: on your interact input, call Trace Button from the camera (start at the camera location, direction along its forward vector, ignore yourself, distance 450 cm by default), then call Press Button on the result with the character's access level.
    4. Weight. A pawn in the cabin counts the car's Passenger Mass Kg (75 by default). Give the pawn a LFLoadComponent to count a different mass.
    5. Access level. Pass the access level of the player to Press Button, Select Floor, Call To Floor and the bank calls. The sample character uses level 0 and the operator panel level 9.

    Multiplayer settings¶

    In multiplayer a custom character needs these settings to ride without jitter or fall-through. They come from the multiplayer tests (listen and dedicated server, 2 clients, 100 ms round trip, 1 % packet loss).

    1. bStayBasedInAir = true on the CharacterMovement component (Jumping / Falling). A jump inside a moving car then stays relative to the car. Client and server compare positions relative to the cabin floor, so corrections are rare, and a car moving up cannot pass through an airborne passenger. StayBasedInAirHeight can stay at its default (1000 cm, more than any cabin).
    2. Nothing else is needed for riding. CharacterMovement makes the car a tick prerequisite of the character automatically, and LiftForge makes the cabin floor and sills network-addressable. Do not move the car to a later tick group and do not drive it from a Timeline.
    3. Impart base velocity (bImpartBaseVelocityX/Y/Z): either setting is fine (the demo uses false). With bStayBasedInAir, no velocity is imparted while you jump inside the car.
    4. MaxStepHeight of 25 cm or more lets the character cross the landing sill smoothly.
    5. Crouch: set NavAgentProps.bCanCrouch on the movement component. Crouching while riding is smooth and replicates.
    6. Sprint: replicate it the usual way (a custom move flag). Changing MaxWalkSpeed on the server and the client independently causes corrections in any game.
    7. Network smoothing: the default (Exponential) is fine. Other players riding with you stay planted on the floor.
    8. A pawn without CharacterMovement (a VR pawn): add LFRiderComponent. It carries the pawn on each machine but does not replicate movement. For multiplayer VR, use a Character-based pawn.
    9. Interactor: each player needs a LFElevatorInteractorComponent. The server adds it automatically. Set its AccessLevel in your game mode's Post Login to unlock restricted floors.
    10. Save the map before multiplayer Play In Editor. See the Multiplayer guide.

    Enhanced Input¶

    The sample pawns use Enhanced Input. You can reuse the assets or use your own.

    Action Type Desktop VR
    IA_LF_Move Axis2D (X right, Y forward) W A S D W A S D and the left thumbstick or trackpad
    IA_LF_Look Axis2D (X yaw, Y pitch) Mouse not used
    IA_LF_Interact Boolean E and left mouse button E and the right trigger
    IA_LF_TogglePanel Boolean H not used
    IA_LF_ToggleVR Boolean V V
    IA_LF_Teleport Boolean not used T and the left trigger
    IA_LF_SnapTurn Axis1D (30 degrees past 0.7) not used Left and Right arrows and the right thumbstick or trackpad X

    The assets are in /LiftForge/Input/ (IA_LF_*, IMC_LF_Desktop, IMC_LF_VR and the data asset DA_LF_DemoInput). To use your own, create a LFDemoInputConfig data asset, fill in your actions and mapping contexts, and select it in Project Settings > Plugins > LiftForge Demo > Input Config. An incomplete asset falls back to the defaults with a warning.

    For your own character you only need your own Interact action: it calls Trace Button and Press Button. You do not have to use the LiftForge input assets at all.

    Spawn your own character in an example map¶

    In World Settings of LF_Showcase_Warm or LF_Showcase_Noir, set GameMode Override to your game mode and Default Pawn Class to your character. The map only uses the demo game mode through World Settings.

    Jitter and fall-through¶

    If the camera shakes in the car or the character sinks, see FAQ and troubleshooting.
