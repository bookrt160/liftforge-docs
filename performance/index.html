    Performance tips¶

    What LiftForge already does¶

    • The geometry is built once per configuration. A hash saved with the actor skips the rebuild at construction and at BeginPlay when nothing changed.
    • Build assets are streamed in at BeginPlay and kept resident.
    • Repeated per-floor fixed parts (rail brackets, shaft lights, door tracks, frames, sills, display housings, hall stations) are instanced, one instanced component per mesh, material and collision group. This happens only when every material of the group has Used with Instanced Static Meshes enabled. All LiftForge master materials and material instances do. If you replace a material with your own, enable that flag on it, or those parts fall back to ordinary components.
    • Generated parts have no overlap events, so the roughly 100 parts of a moving cabin do not run overlap queries.
    • Displays and LED rings update only when their text or lit set changes.
    • Buttons tick only while their press animation plays.
    • A parked car (idle, doors closed, no calls, nothing to announce) ticks every Idle Tick Interval seconds (Project Settings > Plugins > LiftForge, default 0.1). Any call, door command, mode change or fault returns it to every frame. Set 0 to tick every frame.
    • The door light curtain is only read while the doors are not fully closed or while something is bound to On Obstruction Changed.
    • Decorative parts beyond Detail Draw Distance (8000 cm) are hidden. Collision on cabin floors, walls and doors stays on.

    What you can do¶

    Tip How
    Large buildings Use instanced-friendly materials and keep Static Fixed Parts off unless the car never moves at runtime.
    Many cars Let cars park. Do not bind to events you do not use: the light curtain is queried more often when something is bound to the obstruction event.
    Draw distance Lower Detail Draw Distance on the preset for far-away shafts.
    Static lighting Static Fixed Parts gives cheaper fixed geometry but makes the actor's root Static and reports those parts as unbuilt until lighting is built.
    Exposure The example maps have a post process volume with manual exposure. Without one, a scene can render dark because of auto exposure.
    VR Turn on Apply VR Performance CVars (see VR setup). Measure on the headset runtime you target.

    Profiling¶

    Unreal Insights shows CPU trace scopes for ALFElevator::Tick, AnimateParts, UpdateDisplays, UpdateLoad, BuildAssembly and ALFElevatorBank::Tick. Use them to see which part of a car costs time.

    Numbers¶

    Measured on the author's PC (RTX 5090, 2560 x 1440, UE 5.6 editor game session, software Lumen, nine cars moving, no NPCs): median frame time 2.9 to 3.6 ms, 95th percentile 4.9 to 6.3 ms across eight runs on the two maps. Your hardware will differ.

    The test of your own scene¶

    The plugin's -ElevatorBenchmark switch (development builds only) runs a scripted ride and writes a JSON report with frame and game-thread timings. The demo module's benchmark code is compiled out of shipping builds.
